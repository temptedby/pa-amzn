## 2. THE REPORT IS NOT SLOW. WE ARE.

Recovered the true queue time for every report we have ever run from Amazon's own `createdAt` /
`updatedAt`, which needs no waiting at all.

```
hour UTC    n    range                mean
00:00      14    0.4 to  4.8 min      2.6
07-08Z      2    9.1 to 10.7 min
12-13Z      3   28.2 to 31.5 min     29.9
```

The engine runs at 00Z, 06Z, 12Z, 18Z. Its own `engine-mtd` report on 2026-08-17 was
**created 00:01:21Z and COMPLETED 00:01:52Z. Thirty-one seconds**, against a 300-second Vercel budget.
`report-warm` collected it at 00:40:48Z, so the engine waited **38m56s for a report that had been
ready for 38 of them**.

Every "9 to 15 minute queue" figure in the code was measured at the wrong time of day. The whole
deferred request-now-collect-later architecture, the cache key, and therefore the 30-hour staleness
that caused Sunday's overspend, is solving a problem that does not exist at midnight.

**Shipped as PR #5, merged as `23aedee`.** `DATA_STALE_HOURS` 30 to 2, and `getReport` now waits up to
90 seconds inline on both paths. The deferred path is untouched as the slow-case safety net.
`ad-engine.ts`, `ad-rules.ts`, `shouldKill` and `vercel.json` are NOT touched, per William's
instruction not to change the engine. 442 tests pass, tsc clean.

## 10. The bid rule was undoing its own correct cuts

William asked the right question: "were the bids lowered once they were below 2x roas". Yes. Once.

```
retractable phone tether (PHRASE)
  08-15 12:01  DOWN  $0.79 -> $0.69   at 1.63x, correct
  08-16 06:01  UP    $0.69 -> $0.71   "impressions fell 4110 -> 34"
  08-16 18:01  UP    $0.71 -> $0.81
  08-17 06:04  DOWN  $0.81 -> $0.79
  08-17 18:02  UP    $0.79 -> $0.85   ceiling, ABOVE where the cut started
```

The turn-around compared **raw totals from windows of different length**. A window runs from a
keyword's last bid change to now, so a word that had not moved since the 1st carried 14.5 days of
impressions, and the window after its first cut carried twelve hours. Twelve hours cannot beat
fourteen days, so every first cut read as harmful and reversed.

`BidChange` even documented the false assumption in a comment: "both windows are one cooldown long
(~6h), so they are comparable". They never were.

## 11. THE FINDING THAT MATTERS MOST: 570 of 695 moves are raises on noise

Tallied every bid move since 14 August by the reason the engine itself gave:

```
 397  UP    shown, never clicked
 173  UP    never entered the auction
  58  DOWN  turn-around
  46  DOWN  too few clicks, shaving
  15  UP    turn-around
   6  DOWN  under 2x, cutting
```

**Six moves out of 695 were the rule that lowers ACOS.** The turn-around I had just fixed is 73.

The "shown, never clicked" branch raises a bid whenever a keyword has impressions and zero clicks in
the window. The median window it fires on has **7 impressions**, and our account CTR is **0.595%,
one click per 168 impressions**. Seven impressions with no click has about a 96% chance of happening
to a perfectly healthy keyword. The rule reads noise as a verdict and buys on it, 397 times in four
days.

**174 of the 340 keywords we track are already at $0.80 or above.** The climb has largely happened.
