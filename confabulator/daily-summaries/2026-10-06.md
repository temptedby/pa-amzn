## 6. Conversion is U-shaped, not a slope

With 95% confidence intervals, so small samples cannot pose as laws:

```
band          clicks  conv    95% CI
under $0.35       16  12.5%   3.5% to 36.0%   <- too few to mean anything
$0.35-0.60        75   9.3%   4.6% to 18.0%
$0.60-0.90       854   3.6%   2.6% to 5.1%    <- holds $697, the dead band
$0.90-1.40       181   4.4%   2.3% to 8.5%
over $1.40       288  11.1%   8.0% to 15.3%   <- converts BEST
```

The two tight intervals do not overlap: **expensive clicks convert better than mid-priced ones.**
So "cheap clicks convert best" is not what the data says, and I presented 6.71x as a finding before
checking the interval, which was wrong of me.

William's conclusion survives on better grounds. A click is worth conversion x contribution, so
even our best-converting traffic justifies only **$0.55**. Above $0.60 we are always past the line,
whatever the conversion rate.

**We pay 1.27x our own bid.** Weighted bid in force $0.83, CPC paid $1.05, measured against the bid
in force on the day so it is not a stale-bid artefact. Three of ten enabled campaigns run
`AUTO_FOR_SALES`, one of them with +50% on two placements.

**But do not switch dynamic bidding off.** AUTO_FOR_SALES pays $1.39/click, converts 8.3% and
returns 0.68x; everything else pays $0.81, converts 4.1% and returns 0.54x. It earns its premium.
I was one step from recommending the opposite.

### The attribution correction, which was William's catch

I had a list of **172 keywords** ready to switch off, built by applying "not converting" to TOTAL
spend. He said "mind the attribution of 14 days". Splitting the list:

```
active keywords that had never converted      196 words, $551.90
still inside the window, NOT judgeable        177 words, $517.84
fully settled and judgeable                    19 words,  $34.06
$4+ of SETTLED spend and zero sales            25 words, $177.20   <- the real list
```

**90% of what I was about to switch off had not finished reporting.** Two August kills had in fact
converted. It was one command away from happening again.

The fix is structural rather than remembered: `settledWindow()` ends 14 days ago, so nothing inside
the window can be judged at all, and a keyword with no settled row is skipped entirely because
silence is not a verdict.

## 10. The mistake I made, and the invariant it bought

Enabling the 3-Pack campaign switched on a word I had retired four hours earlier. EXACT "phone
tether retractable" sat ENABLED **inside** the paused campaign, so turning the campaign on made it
serve at $2.50, over cap and ceiling. My script only *skipped* it when re-bidding; skipping leaves
the state alone, and the state was already ENABLED.

Caught by the script's own read-back printing `VIOLATION: 1`, fixed about three minutes later.
`budget-usage` confirms **$0.00 spent**, so it cost nothing, but it could have been a $2.50 click.

"Off because the container is off" is not off. The daily sweep now enforces **a tombstoned word is
never ENABLED** and reports a `zombies` count that should sit at 0.
