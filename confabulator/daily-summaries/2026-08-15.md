## Six rule changes, all on William's corrections

**1. A Display cut was the entire bid.** `SD_BID_STEP` was set to a nickel on the 13th when Display
entry was still a dime. Entry moved to $0.05 on the 14th and the nickel became 100% of the bid: cut
$0.05 by $0.05, want $0.00, clamp to Amazon's $0.02 minimum. Four targets went from the entry bid to
the absolute floor in one run, four hours after William set them. `SD_CUT_STEP` is now two cents.

**2. Every step halved.** *"If five cents or ten cents is too much of a move, try five or two."*
Ten-cent moves are now five, five-cent moves are now two, including the step above $2.50. Still flat
cents, never a percentage.

**3. The turn-around was reversing moves that were never worse.** It compared a keyword's
whole-month impressions against the six hours since its bid last moved. A month always beats six
hours, so it read a collapse that had not happened. **59 of 82 moves in a live dry run were
triggered this way**, every one about to undo a cut that had just been made correctly.
`kw_bid_history` gains `window_hours`, the comparison is per hour, and with a length missing on
either side it does not fire at all.

**4. The reintroduction shield was blocking the cuts William asked for.** Three of the six re-enabled
words could not be cut because they had been woken by the reintroduction job days earlier and carry
a 14-day shield. They had 10 to 13 clicks and real orders each. The gate is now SPEND, on William's
instruction, which effectively retires the shield. Backed by the 08-13 measurement that attribution
lands on day one, not day fourteen.

**5. The whole rule in one file.** `rule-tree.test.ts` states his four branches directly rather than
leaving them to be inferred across three files, with the edges pinned: $3.99 does not stop, exactly
1.0x survives, every ratio from 1.01x to 9x is cut rather than paused.

```
has not spent             -> raise, it is not in the auction
spent $4+, no conversion  -> stop
converted, under 1.0x     -> stop
converted, 1.0x to 2.0x   -> lower the bid only, never raise
converted, 2.0x and above -> raise AND lower, hunting the best ROAS
```

**6. Above 2x the search goes both ways.** *"we raise and lower bids if over 2x trying to max roas
but keep above 2x."* The no-raise rule was absolute and sat inside `move()`, which is exactly what
stopped a 3x word climbing back after a cut. Now scoped: absolute below the target, two-directional
above it. "Keep above 2x" is enforced by the loop rather than by prediction, and it currently has
nothing to act on because the best word on the account is 1.63x.

Live dry run after all six: 53 moves, 28 up, 25 down, step sizes 2c and 5c only, no stops, no
spurious turn-arounds.
