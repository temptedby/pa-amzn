## 4. THE FINDING, corrected: the bid ladder raises a word BECAUSE it did not spend

`ladderVerdict()` is one line of logic: `if (s.spendSinceStep > 0) return { action: "hold" }`. A word
that gets no clicks climbs a rung. Performance never enters. There is no ROAS term in it at all.

`kw_bid_state` holds **430 keywords with `ladder_active = 1`, average bid $0.73, and 243 already
parked at the $0.85 ceiling.** 216 carry an `escalated_at` stamp, which is the flag that means
"reached the ceiling, William notified".

I first stated this as one group and William caught it. The 430 split in two and they behave nothing
alike:

```
                          words   Aug spend    ROAS   orders
ladder words that spent     127     $502.90    0.48x      21
ladder words that did not   303       $0.00       -        0
```

And at the top rung specifically: **243 words sit at $0.85, only 31 of them have ever spent a cent,
$94.27 between them.** That is exactly what the rule produces. A word climbs BECAUSE it is idle, so
the ceiling fills with idle words, and the spending happens on the way up and after one finally
catches clicks.

Against the rest of the account, whole month, Sponsored Products, our archive at $875.97 against
Amazon's $879.61:

```
                words   spend     sales   ROAS  orders
ON THE LADDER     127  $502.90  $241.27  0.48x      21
everything else   146  $373.07  $310.21  0.83x      29
```

57.4% of Sponsored Products spend, at 0.48x, on the cohort whose bids were raised for not spending.

**And it is loaded. $1,212 of unused $4 rope sits on the 303 that have not spent, $903.34 of it on
words already at maximum bid.** That is the answer to why the kill rule cannot reach the money. The
bar is not wrong. We keep issuing fresh $4 allowances at the top bid to words with no evidence.

in-month revival owns it and a word that attribution lifts back over 2.15x comes back on its own.
Run dry with a cached report first so the live pass is instant.
