## 8. Built: the BACOS governor

`src/lib/amazon/bacos.ts`, 27 tests, suite at **564**, typecheck clean. Two-way, as William asked
("i like throttle idea if things are going well then press the spend"):

```
BACOS over 40%        throttle, step spend DOWN 20%
BACOS under 32%       press,    step spend UP   10%
between 32 and 40%    hold -- a dead band so it cannot oscillate
```

Steps are unequal deliberately: down is firm because 81% BACOS costs ~$20 of contribution a day, up
is gentle per [[cautious-step-on-what-works]]. One step takes $43.93 to $35.14 and reaches the
$21.67 allowance in four days. It refuses to act on nothing: zero revenue returns "undefined" and
holds rather than reading a reporting gap as infinitely bad.

**This supersedes William's 2026-08-28 call** to leave daily budgets alone because hourly enforcement
would be the brake. Hourly enforcement never became one, and section 3 shows why it cannot be.

### The live dry run earned its keep three times

```
BACOS 79.3%   ACTION: THROTTLE
current $48.83/day   allowed $24.64/day   this move $39.07/day
10 budget moves, every one downward
authorised budget $747.00/day -> $59.01/day
```

The FIRST version of this would have **raised the 3-Pack test campaign from $2.00 to $5.00 inside a
throttle**, because the per-campaign floor was applied as a share, and it authorised $82.01/day
against a $39.07 plan because nine idle campaigns each took the floor. Three rules now:

1. Only campaigns that actually spent get repriced. A dormant campaign is not contributing to BACOS
   and rewriting it breaks deliberate setups.
2. A dormant campaign is pulled **down** to a $5 cap so it cannot wake up holding $100. Capping at
   the planned daily spend was the first attempt and was worse, leaving it at $39.07.
3. A throttle may never raise a budget; a press may never lower one.

The spender floor also dropped $5 -> $1, Amazon's real minimum, because $5 on five campaigns whose
combined share was $2.05 authorised twelve times the plan.

It also stopped depending on the report queue. The first harness asked for a 7-day spend report and
died after **900s still PENDING**; reading `kw_day` / `ad_day_observation` instead runs in **4
seconds**. A governor that runs daily cannot block on that queue.

One stated gap: **Brands and Display are not in `ad_day_observation`**, so their $18.63 is passed in
by hand. Treating it as zero would flatter BACOS and invite more spend, so the script refuses to run
silently without it and labels the result a floor.
