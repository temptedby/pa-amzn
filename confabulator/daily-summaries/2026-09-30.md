## 2. THE FINDING: past conversion predicts nothing

William asked whether any of last month's dead words converted this month. Seventeen did. The
control group is what matters.

```
                    retried in Sept   converted   rate    Sept ROAS
converted in Aug          27              5      18.5%      0.73x
zero in Aug               97             17      17.5%      0.56x
```

**A word that converted in August was no more likely to convert in September than one that never
converted at all.** 22 of 27 proven earners produced nothing. Click volume is not predictive either,
and runs slightly backwards (Aug 1-2 clicks gave 0.773x, Aug 11+ gave 0.619x).

At 0.59x against a 2.42x break-even, every dollar of ad spend returns about 24 cents of
contribution. Every keyword draw loses 76% of its cost whatever its history. The 17 that converted
were the second flip of the same coin.

Tombstone backtest: blocking August's 97 zero-sale words would have saved $368.43 and given up
$86.03 of contribution. **Net +$282.40.** The gain comes from spending less, not choosing better;
the blocked pool returned 0.564x against an account average of 0.590x.

### The one variable that does predict, using prior-month data only

```
Aug CPC band      retried   Sept ROAS
under $0.40           3       8.60x   (too small to trust)
$0.40-0.70           14       0.72x
$0.70-1.00           60       0.60x
over $1.00           47       0.43x
```

What we pay per click predicts next month. What the word did last month does not.

### The two-strikes list

80 words with zero sales in August AND zero again in September: **$470.38 across both months for
$0.00.** 41 already paused, 39 still enabled at $0.81-0.85 bids. Two are our own brand terms. Three
are not our product (`lanyards for id badges`, `tethers for remote controls`, `key chain

## 6. RBB: the watchdog, and my wrong claim about it

I claimed the watchdog could not prove it was watching. **That was wrong**, and an authenticated
dry run against production proved it:

```
clean = False   heartbeat due = True   violations 0   unread 1
  "US Brands: sb/keywords 406"
```

The heartbeat writer is correct. It is gated on `clean` (`route.ts:73`), and one 406 has made every
run un-clean, so the condition was never satisfiable. Worse: because `clean` is false the route
never takes its silent path (`route.ts:44`) and **has been alerting every hour, 24 a day, about the
same 406.**

Reproduced live, both header sets against the real endpoint:

```
HTTP 406  engine-watch.ts:273  Content-Type: application/json + Accept: application/json
HTTP 200  sb-v2.ts:198         Accept: application/vnd.sbkeyword.v3+json, NO Content-Type   670 rows
```

`sb-v2.ts:203` also uses `parsePreservingIds`; `engine-watch.ts:282` uses plain `res.json()`, so a
header-only fix would still give rounded 18-digit ids that match nothing.

Recommended: reuse `fetchSbKeywords`, and ungate the heartbeat from `clean` so liveness is reported
regardless of health. What NOT to do: make `isClean` ignore unread scopes. Not built, awaiting a go.
