## The RBB William asked for: duplicate keywords

*"what amazon says about the same key words in multiple ads."* Written up in
`RBB-duplicate-keywords-2026-08-07.md`.

**Amazon does not let you bid against yourself.** Only one of your eligible ads enters any given
auction, chosen on bid *and* relevance, in a second-price auction. Duplicates do not inflate CPC.
Ad Badger, FlyRank and Scalea2z all claim otherwise; Ad Badger cites no Amazon source at all.

Tested on our own account rather than picking a blog. 260 enabled groups share a (text, match type),
covering 605 keywords. Of the groups that spent anything this month:

```
  5 groups — ALL spend landed on ONE copy
  0 groups — spend split across more than one copy
```

**This disproved my own objection.** I had argued spend spreads thinly so no copy ever reaches $4
while the word bleeds. It concentrates on the highest-bid copy, which then reaches $4 on its own.
William's per-keyword rule works because of how Amazon actually behaves.

Caveat kept in the doc: only 5 groups had spend, on $13.16 of SP spend in 7 days. Directional, not
proof. Re-run at month end.

## The bid engine, rebuilt three times in one evening

William killed two designs before the third was right, and he was correct each time.

1. *Cooldown 7 days + roll back a bad raise.* — "every 7 days thats wild??" Fair: I had used one
   number for two different clocks.
2. *Hill climb with turn-arounds.* — "no thats not how you max roas you have to spend to max roas."
3. **What shipped:** *"just have to try to keep ads spending and roas above 2x that's the goal."*

```
  not spending    -> UP.   Not in the auction. Waiting for evidence it cannot
                           produce is how 1,830 words parked at $0.10. ".10 is not ok"
  spending, >=2x  -> UP.   Profitable, so buy more of it.
  spending, <2x   -> DOWN. Paying too much per sale.
```

It converges on its own: climbs until ROAS falls through 2x, drops back, settles at the highest bid
the word can carry while still paying for itself. No direction memory needed — the 2x line steers.

**Step size, corrected by William:** *"i asked raising by .10 not 10%"* — a flat ten cents, the same
step the reintroduction ladder uses. A percentage step is worst exactly where it is needed most: 10%
of a $0.10 bid is one cent, so a floored keyword needs **19 runs** to reach the $0.59 market CPC. A
flat dime gets there in **five**.

**A real bug this caught:** the step guard compared dollars as floats, so `$0.10 -> $0.11` read as
"no change" and returned null. That pinned every floored keyword at $0.10 — precisely the trap the
rule exists to escape. All arithmetic is now in whole cents.

Live dry run after the fix: **73 bid moves, all floored keywords climbing out** ($0.10 -> $0.20,
$0.34 -> $0.44). Before it, 1.
