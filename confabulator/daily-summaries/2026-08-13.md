## A test failed and the failure was the finding

I asserted the 70/30 blend would refuse to raise a bid on one lucky click. It does not:

```
one click, one $9.49 order in 6h  = 10.5x
trailing 13 days                  =  1.63x
blended at 25% weight             =  3.86x  -> still reads "rising"
```

No constant fixes that; the ratio is too extreme. So weighting is necessary and **not sufficient**,
and the real guard is an asymmetry: raising spends more money so it must be earned, cutting spends
less so it never needs permission. William then made it stricter still — a spending word is never
raised at all, enforced inside `move()` so the turn-around cannot route around it.

## The bid history that could never have worked

`kw_bid_history` was created with a column `acos_before`. The engine has always INSERTed
`roas_before`. SQLite rejects the statement and the engine catches it into `out.errors`, where nobody
reads it. **Every bid-history write has failed silently since the table existed**, which is why it
holds zero rows and why the turn-around rule has never once fired.

Proven by replaying the engine's exact INSERT against the live database, not by reading the code
twice. Replaced with `bid_epoch`: one row per bid level with what that level produced, so "did
lowering help?" becomes a query instead of a guess.

## Attribution lands on day one, not day fourteen

Measured on a fully settled July period:

```
sales1d $48.96   sales7d $48.96   sales14d $48.96   sales30d $48.96
```

Identical at every horizon. **Every sale was attributed within one day of the click.** So
`REINTRO_PROTECT_DAYS = 14` waits fourteen times longer than the evidence supports, and my earlier
argument that short windows read low because sales are in flight was wrong — I had conflated the
attribution window with report availability. The noise argument stands on its own: 152 clicks in 13

## Display: not mis-targeted, bid up

I switched 42 ads live on a **stock** decision, not a performance one. William pushed back — *"how
did you get ads back live and decide which ones to go live?"* — and he was right to. Reversed 40 of
42, precisely, using `creationDate` from the extended endpoint after my first reversal logic would

Then the lifetime data, and the thing the blended averages hid:

```
views 14d  $0.10 bid ->   122 clicks  2.41x      views 30d  $0.10 -> 1,664 clicks  1.16x
views 14d  $0.48 bid ->   411 clicks  0.67x      views 30d  $0.34 ->   721 clicks  0.47x
```

**Same audience. The low bid wins every time.** Amazon suggests $3.52-$5.56 on these. I had
recommended pausing views 30d at a blended 0.71x; at ten cents it returns 1.16x on the largest click
sample in the account. William's instinct to look at the bid caught my error.

The two families also run in opposite directions, which is consistent with a one-per-phone durable:

```
VIEWS      shorter is better    60d 0.57x -> 30d 0.71x -> 14d 1.38x
PURCHASES  LONGER is better     90d 0.00x -> 180d 0.34x -> 365d 0.57x
```

**Probed live and settled:** views accepts 7/14/30 and rejects 3; purchases accepts 90/180/365 and
rejects 270. Seven days is the floor and there is no 270-day window. All six probe targets were
created paused and archived again.

**Applied to the account:** 15 non-performers paused, 16 of 17 survivors re-bid to $0.10. One refused
— `"Cannot update bid ... when the ad group has an enabled optimization rule"` — Amazon's own
optimiser is holding that group at $1.67.
