## A test failed and the failure was the finding

I asserted the 70/30 blend would refuse to raise a bid on one lucky click. It does not:

```
one click, one $9.49 order in 6h  = 10.5x
trailing 13 days                  =  1.63x
blended at 25% weight             =  3.86x  -> still reads "rising"
```

No constant fixes that; the ratio is too extreme. So weighting is necessary and **not sufficient**,
and the real guard is an asymmetry: raising spends more money so it must be earned, cutting spends
less so it never needs permission. William then made it stricter still — a spending word is never
raised at all, enforced inside `move()` so the turn-around cannot route around it.

## The bid history that could never have worked

`kw_bid_history` was created with a column `acos_before`. The engine has always INSERTed
`roas_before`. SQLite rejects the statement and the engine catches it into `out.errors`, where nobody
reads it. **Every bid-history write has failed silently since the table existed**, which is why it
holds zero rows and why the turn-around rule has never once fired.

Proven by replaying the engine's exact INSERT against the live database, not by reading the code
twice. Replaced with `bid_epoch`: one row per bid level with what that level produced, so "did
lowering help?" becomes a query instead of a guess.

## Attribution lands on day one, not day fourteen

Measured on a fully settled July period:

```
sales1d $48.96   sales7d $48.96   sales14d $48.96   sales30d $48.96
```

Identical at every horizon. **Every sale was attributed within one day of the click.** So
`REINTRO_PROTECT_DAYS = 14` waits fourteen times longer than the evidence supports, and my earlier
argument that short windows read low because sales are in flight was wrong — I had conflated the
attribution window with report availability. The noise argument stands on its own: 152 clicks in 13
days across 2,279 keywords.

views 14d  $0.48 bid ->   411 clicks  0.67x      views 30d  $0.34 ->   721 clicks  0.47x

PURCHASES  LONGER is better     90d 0.00x -> 180d 0.34x -> 365d 0.57x

# Evening session — the rejection emails, and six targets that cannot serve

## What William asked

Six emails had landed at hello@ saying there were issues with the ads, and his read was that
something had changed an image that was already approved. Then: get them approved, swap in images
that qualify, show them in a browser, and can we do video or only image.

## Nothing changed an image

No code in this repo has ever called a creative endpoint. Every Display write we make is
`/sd/productAds` (state, create) or `/sd/targets` (state, bid), confirmed by grep across 95 scripts
and `sd-engine.ts`. The account agrees: all nine Display creatives report `assetVersion: version_v1`,
uploaded once and never replaced.

What actually happened is a re-review. At 17:41 UTC I created 42 Display product ads; 21 landed in
six ad groups whose custom image is a 2023 infographic panel. Adding an ad to an ad group makes
Amazon re-moderate that ad group's image. Six ad groups, six emails, 18:00 to 18:39, each listing
three `CUSTOM_IMAGE` violations, which is the square, horizontal and vertical crop of one file.

The images do fail policy. Display custom images may carry no added text, graphics, inset images or
borders:

| Image | Campaign | What is on it |
|---|---|---|
| UNMATCHED DURABILITY & SECURITY | Pro Retarget | headline, 3 bullets, 2 dashed-arrow labels, 2 inset circles |
| RETRACTABLE SMARTPHONE SAFETY LEASH AND CLIP | Black Combo Retarget | blue text box over a split lifestyle shot |

Amazon's own moderation is inconsistent about this: a four-panel collage in `3 Pack Retarget` is
APPROVED, while the infographic is not.

## The correction that mattered

I first said the images had been rejected "19 months ago". William asked how I knew. I did not. I
had inferred it from an ad creation date of 2025-01-11 plus a current `servingStatus`, which carries
no timestamp. What I had actually measured was 30 days of zero impressions.

The real evidence is `campaign_lifetime`, from Amazon's own `Campaign_Aug_5_2026.csv` export:

```
Black 1 retarget       01/11/2025   2,144 clicks   $2,136.98   $1,168.38 sales
2 Pack Retarget        01/11/2025     649 clicks     $680.54     $553.52
3 Pack Retarget        01/11/2025     184 clicks      $86.30      $53.90
Pro Retarget           01/11/2025       0 clicks       $0.00       $0.00
Black Combo Retarget   01/11/2025       0 clicks       $0.00       $0.00
```

The two campaigns with rejected images have never taken a click or spent a cent since January 2025.
A rejection *date* is still not available: Amazon exposes no moderation timestamp, and v3 reporting
only retains back to 2026-06-10, so the daily series cannot reach 2025 either.

## Why this is worth fixing

Mapping every enabled retarget target to its ad group's creative status:

```
REJECTED   views 14d       Pro Retarget          REJECTED   views 14d       Black Combo
REJECTED   views 30d       Pro Retarget          REJECTED   views 30d       Black Combo
REJECTED   purchases 365d  Pro Retarget          REJECTED   purchases 365d  Black Combo
```

Six of the fifteen enabled retarget targets sit behind a rejected image. That is a complete
duplicate set of exactly the audiences William says the data now supports, short-window views and
long-window buyers, on the two products the structure currently cannot reach. Fixing the image
roughly doubles the surface area of yesterday's restructure.

**ad group**, and all six read `IMAGE`. Video would need new ad groups, which means the audiences
restart learning. We hold 42 videos in `assets/source/_drive-video` plus the finished 2022 Amazon ad
cut, so the material exists.
