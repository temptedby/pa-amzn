# 2026-08-08 — all three ad products get the $4 kill, and reports stop being the bottleneck

Morning review found the engine healthy and doing very little. The day ended with every ad product
under one rule, every spend judged on its own, and the report queue no longer setting the pace.

## The headline

**Sponsored Display had never been governed by anything.** It is the worst performer in the
account: $4,546.85 spent since 2019 against $3,976.33 of sales, a 0.87x return against a 1.92x
break-even, so it returns less than half what it costs. Sponsored Products has had the $4 rule for
weeks and Sponsored Brands got it on 08-07. Display was still running on $200/day of authorised
budget with no rule at all. It has one now.

## Morning review

Crons fired on schedule: ad-engine at 00:00 and 06:00Z, reintroduction at 06:30Z.

**What was working.** The reintroduction job promoted 30 keywords from $0.10 to $0.25 on lifetime
evidence, 10 per run. The $4 kill fired twice and both landed, verified by reading state back from
Amazon rather than trusting the log: `phone tether` PHRASE on 08-07 and `hand strap universal phone
lanyard clip to belt` BROAD on 08-08.

**What was not.**

1. The en-dash keyword was still being submitted twice a run. Zero copies exist on the account and
   it had been logged `applied=1` fourteen times since 08-06.
2. The floor trap had not moved: 1,810 of 2,281 enabled keywords bid $0.10 or less, 79%.
   Production was still the old percentage engine, making one bid change per run.
3. The kill overshoots. It fired at $6.95 by the engine's reading on a keyword whose real
   month-to-date was $13.61, because the report it judges lags about a day.

All three were fixed inside PR #2, which was open and unmerged.

## William's rules, applied

Four instructions, in the order they arrived.

**"we need 40 a day not 20."** The cause was Amazon's report queue: a run that requests a window
cannot also collect it, and the old code returned empty whenever any window was outstanding, so
roughly every other run promoted nothing. Promotion now falls back to lifetime evidence from
`kw_lifetime` when the window is late. The safety the block existed for is kept: without the window
a word that recently spent badly would read as "never spent", so any candidate holding a lifetime
record that fails the 2x bar is skipped for that run rather than guessed at. What promotes is
exactly the two groups named: proven 2x+ winners, and words with no spending record at all.

**"launch key words that are low in spend that 2x roas or higher first."** This reverses the 08-06
rule, which led on lifetime sales volume. A word returning 2x on $3 is an unfinished experiment; one
returning 2x on $900 has already had its run. Verified against `kw_lifetime`, the live batch now
launches in strictly ascending order of lifetime spend: $0.42, $1.00, $1.49, $1.70, $1.95, $2.08,
$2.24, $2.26, $2.32, $2.66.

**"max bids go to $.85 then notified."** I had removed the $0.85 ceiling earlier in the day, reading
"until $4 is hit" as licence to let the ladder climb to BID_CAP on the reasoning that the $4 kill
bounded the downside. That was wrong and was reverted. The per-run stepping stands: a dime every 6
hours instead of a dime a day, which reaches the $0.59 market CPC in about 30 hours instead of five
days.

**"Each spend is individual."** This caught a real defect in Sponsored Brands. `sb-engine.ts` was
aggregating month-to-date by (text, match type) and pausing every enabled copy together. It now
groups by keyword id and pauses only the id that spent the money, matching Sponsored Products.

## Sponsored Display, built and verified

`src/lib/amazon/sd-engine.ts`, new cron at `45 */6`.

Judged per TARGET, which is where the bid lives and is the exact analogue of a keyword. Same shared
`shouldKill()` as the other two products. Probed live before a line was written: `/sd/targets` 200
(104 targets, 81 enabled), `/sd/productAds` 200 (129, 88 enabled), `/sd/campaigns` 200 (14, 8
enabled). Display returns state strings lowercase, unlike Products, and there is a test pinning that.

Deliberately not judged: the advertised ASIN. It carries the same dollars seen from another angle,
so acting on both would judge one dollar twice, and pausing a product ad stops advertising that
product outright rather than trimming what is not working.

First live end-to-end read: 28 targets with rows, 5 with spend, $3.69 month-to-date, $0.00 sales,
nothing past the $4 bar, nothing paused. Correct.

**Worth saying plainly:** at roughly $3.69 a month across all of Display, a single target will
rarely reach $4 inside one calendar month. This rule is a guardrail against Display scaling up
again. It is not going to claw back the 0.87x lifetime loss on its own.

## The report latency, and what it actually was

Two Display report probes read "still pending" after 550 seconds and I nearly wrote the path off.
Both were COMPLETED when polled again later:

```
a84b8047  created 11:27:54Z  completed 11:37:25Z   (9m31s)  28 rows
5e4572fc  created 12:35:58Z  completed 12:50:05Z  (14m07s)  18 rows
```

`targetingId` is present and non-null on every row. **Sponsored Display takes 10 to 15 minutes where
Sponsored Products takes about 9.** A pending SD report is not a broken SD report, and any inline
poll under about 15 minutes will look like a failure.

**The fix, on William's push that 40 minutes is too long:** a warm-up cron at `40 */6` that requests
every report the engines will need and does nothing else. It never decides and never writes to the
account. Each engine then finds its reports already COMPLETED and acts on roughly 20-minute-old data
instead of the 6-hour-old data the deferred pattern produced on its own.

```
  :40   report-warm            <- requests everything
  :00   ad-engine              (20 min later)
  :15   sb-engine              (35 min)
  :30   ad-engine-reintroduce  (50 min)
  :45   sd-engine              (65 min)
```

To make that safe, the report spec is now built in exactly one place per product, so the warm-up job
and the engine cannot drift into different cache keys. Live proof: the warm-up found **8 ready, 0
newly requested**. A single changed column name would have requested 8 fresh reports instead.

Sponsored Brands is not warmed, on purpose. It does not use the v3 queue at all; `sb-v2.ts` pulls
legacy day reports synchronously, so there is no queue to get ahead of.


## Amazon Attribution and the Brand Referral Bonus

**UTM parameters were the wrong tool and would have tracked nothing.** Nothing reads them: Google
Analytics cannot run on Amazon's pages, Amazon does not return query strings to sellers, and a bare
domain forward has no page of ours in between to observe the click. The equivalent that works is an
Amazon Attribution tag, whose campaign and ad group names serve as `utm_source` and `utm_medium`.

Attribution was already enabled: advertiser **Phone Assured**, id `593385895532730388`.

**The tag, verified by loading it:**

```
https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07Y5GZP1T?maas=maas_adg_5E2C5122203A35D8F4072F77A70C26B5_afap_abs&ref_=aa_maas&tag=maas
```

It resolves to the flagship, $9.49, in stock, with the `maas` parameters intact. Amazon appends its
own `&th=1`, which is harmless. Short `a.co` links are REJECTED by Attribution; the one William
tried resolves to this same ASIN.

**Brand Referral Bonus: ENROLLED**, confirmed by the green "You are enrolled!" badge, not by an
acknowledgment. It pays a credit against future referral fees, averaging ~10% and ranging roughly
6.5-11.2% by category, capped at the referral fee on the transaction and paid two months in arrears.

Enrollment was blocked twice on tax information, and the reason is worth recording: **there are
three unrelated tax records.** INFORM Tax Verification (already Verified), the advertising-side tax
info William had completed, and the Seller Central **Tax Information Interview (W-9)** at
`sellercentral/tax-interview/landing`, which is the one BRB requires. Completing that one enrolled
him automatically.

**Qualified Traffic is narrower than "any link".** Per the terms: traffic must be tied to an
Attribution tag AND land on a detail page or Store page you own in Brand Registry.

**Affiliate networks conflict with this.** The BRB terms state BRB and Amazon Associates "should not
be combined simultaneously", and claiming both on the same traffic risks "terminating all involved
selling account and Amazon Associates account". A ShareASale-style programme pushing Associates
links directly conflicts with the bonus enrolled today. Paying affiliates from our own margin is the
only compatible shape, and on $9.49 with $4.93 of contribution there is very little to share.
