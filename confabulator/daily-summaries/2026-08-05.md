# 2026-08-05 — the day we found out the winners were switched off

## The headline

**151 profitable keywords are paused or archived. They hold $46,283 of lifetime sales.
274 more are enabled and bidding $0.11 or less.**

That is the answer to why sales collapsed, and it is not indexing, copy, price or market.
The ad engine walked the account's proven winners down to the floor and then paused them, one
10% cut at a time, because every rule it applies judges a **month** and these keywords convert
on a longer cycle than that.

## How the day started: a number that was wrong by 10x

Reported August spend as **$5.99**. William pushed back: "we have spent over $50 this month."
He was right. The $5.99 was Sponsored Products, US profile only. The Sponsored Display report had
errored silently and Sponsored Brands was never queried at all.

His screenshot settled it: **$57.76 for Aug 1-4, of which one SB campaign spent $50.78.**

### Why the API kept saying zero

Sponsored Brands reports for this profile either return COMPLETED with zero rows, or never complete.
Three shapes (`sbCampaigns` SUMMARY, `sbAdGroup`, `sbSearchTerm`) sat at PENDING for **29+ minutes**.
SP and SD reports on the same profile in the same hour finish in ~9 minutes.

This is the same stalled path that produced the "SB spent $0.00 in July" conclusion on 2026-08-02,
which was written into memory as a *correction* and then reasoned from for three days. The original
2026-07-24 hypothesis — that unmanaged Sponsored Brands was the leak — was right all along.

**Workaround found:** `POST /sb|sp|sd/campaigns/budget/usage` returns HTTP 200 with
`budgetUsagePercent` and a timestamp. It bypasses the report queue entirely and gives spend, though
not sales. The account's day resets at **07:00 UTC** (midnight Pacific), which the timestamps reveal.

## Control and visibility, verified with real writes

| | Spend | Sales/ACOS | Write |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sponsored Products | yes | yes | **verified** (keyword bid, item-level success) |
| Sponsored Display | yes | yes | **verified** |
| Sponsored Brands | **via budget usage only** | **no** | **verified** |

The SP campaign write that failed did so with `UPDATING_ENDED_CAMPAIGN_WITHOUT_EXTENSION` — that
campaign has a past end date. Not a permission gap, but it means bids pushed into ended campaigns
are silently refused.

## Amazon's data retention, measured not assumed

`startDate must be equal to or after report type data retention start date (2026-05-02)` — quoted
from the API. **95 days for Sponsored Products, and SD only goes back to 2026-06-01.** Everything
from 120 days to 2 years back was rejected. True lifetime is not available through the API at all.

Our own code was using a 65-day window, which was *our* choice, not Amazon's limit. Thirty days of
free evidence was being discarded.

## So we built our own database

| table | rows | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| `kw_daily` | 4,433 | 95 days of daily keyword performance, backfilled before it rolls off |
| `kw_lifetime` | ~1,900 | lifetime totals per word, with current bid and Amazon's suggested bid |
| `campaign_lifetime` | 50 | every campaign since 2019 |
| `ad_entity_lifetime` | 76 | SD audiences, SD targets, SP and SD ads |
| `kw_state_snapshot` | 3,458 | every keyword's bid and state, as a rollback point |

Two reusable ingest tools were written, `scripts/ingest-keyword-csv.mjs` and
`scripts/ingest-sd-csv.mjs`. They normalise Amazon's header names (which differ per report, per year
and per currency), aggregate before writing so re-running a file can never double-count, and route
dated rows to daily history and undated rows to lifetime totals.

Validation: July in `kw_daily` reads **$82.09**, matching an independent report pull made hours
earlier through a different code path.

## The lifetime picture, from William's console exports

**$100,053.57 spent since 2019. $171,744.34 in sales. 9,175 orders. Blended 1.72x.**

Break-even is **1.92x** (52% ACOS, validated from real fees: $9.49 price, $0.62 COGS, $1.42 referral,
$2.52 FBA). So over seven years and a hundred thousand dollars, **the ads have never been profitable
in aggregate**, and not by a wide margin — consistently about 11% short.

**Only 4 of 50 campaigns clear break-even, and they hold $1,302 of spend. 1.3%.** All four are
branded. $98,751 went into campaigns that lose money on every sale.

Yet the account contains **444 individually profitable keywords worth $70,560 of sales**. The
portfolio loses money while its best parts win. That is a management failure, not a market verdict.

## Why the $4 rule did not stop it

Traced day by day from `kw_daily`:

| keyword | rule should have fired | spent after |
|---|---|---|
| phone tethered | 2026-07-07 | $8.27 |
| phone tether | 2026-07-13 | $6.80 |

And the pattern in what it *did* kill: **all three logged kills had zero orders. Every keyword it
missed had at least one.** The rule catches words that never sell and lets through words that sell
at a loss, which is the expensive group. `phone tether` ran at 103% ACOS and was never touched.

Separately: the reintroduction collector is **broken**. Its reports have been stuck `REQUESTED`
since 2026-08-03 and `ad_reintro_cohort` has 0 rows, so the floor-trap fix has never promoted a
single keyword.

## Rules written and tested

`ad-rules.ts` gained, with **174 tests passing**:

- reintroduction entry bid **$0.50 → $0.25**
- **+$0.10 per day of zero spend, ceiling $0.85**, then `escalate` to Telegram rather than auto-raise
- `isPermanentlyDead` — $4 spent with zero orders is tombstoned for good, because a 95-day window
  makes a killed keyword look untested again by month four
- `shouldRetirePermanently` — 3 consecutive months of $4-with-no-conversion retires even a past
  winner, with calendar gaps breaking the run

## The rollout plan, not yet applied

Three waves of 26 keywords, two days apart, bid **10% below Amazon's suggested low** and capped by
each keyword's own break-even. Waves assigned round-robin down the sales ranking so they are
comparable. Worst case if nothing converts: **$312 for the month**, $104 for wave one.

**Two things must happen before wave one:** duplicates of the same word must move together (three
copies of `retractable phone leash` currently span all three waves and would bid against each
other), and the cohort must be protected from the automated bid cut, which would otherwise walk them
back to the floor within days.

Nothing was applied. `kw_state_snapshot` holds every bid and state at `as_of='2026-08-05'`.
