# Discreet — the positioning spine for all four SKUs

v1, 2026-08-06. William's direction, 2026-08-05 and 2026-08-06.
Sits under [PACR](PACR-phone-assured-creative-rules.md) as a pre-build gate. Every image, video,
A+ module and line of copy is checked against this before it is made.

-----

## The direction, in William's words

> "black is for descrte peace of mind we have to flip the lightweight to descrete and easily
> perserved"

> "dont need a strong bulky clip for light phones need to feel security without feeling security
> on you intrusive"

So: **lightweight is retired as a claim. Discreet replaces it.** Lightweight describes the object.
Discreet describes what the customer gets. Nobody buys a phone tether because it is light.

-----

## Why this is the right axis, commercially

From the 2026-08-04 competitor teardown:

| Competitor | Rating | Reviews | Price | Leads with |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rogue Fishing Co. | 4.6 | 2,300 | $21.99 | ANTI-THEFT, steel cable |
| BPZZ Cut-Resistant | 4.2 | 19 | $13.99 | ANTI-THEFT, cut-resistant steel |
| SUPBEE Anti-Theft | 3.8 | 79 | ~$11.99 | ANTI-THEFT, steel cord |
| **Phone Assured** | **3.8** | **480** | **$9.49** | retractable tether |

Every one of them sells **visible strength**. Thick cable, industrial hardware, the message that
you are carrying protection. We are the cheapest of ten and the worst selling, so **price is not
the problem** and out-arming them on cable thickness is a fight we lose with the product we hold.

Discretion is the one axis where nobody is standing. It is also the only axis our actual hardware
wins on, which makes it defensible rather than aspirational.

-----

## The fact that makes it true

From the confirmed product facts:

> **Purpose of retraction: discretion. No visible cord hanging off you.**

This is the whole argument. A steel cable hangs off your hip and announces itself. Our cord
retracts into its housing, so between uses there is nothing to see. The competitor's product is
worn. Ours disappears.

And the soft spring, which reads as a weakness in every return comment, is what makes it possible:
a spring strong enough to haul a phone back up would need hardware you would notice all day, and it
can snap back on a finger. **Soft spring is what buys the discretion.** Same fact, correct frame.

-----

## The line

**Security you feel, not security you wear.**

Supporting: it clips, it disappears, it is there when you drop.

## The claim, in William's words (2026-08-06)

> "its enough for safety but not too bulky for your everyday clip"

This is the sentence the whole position turns on, and it is better than "discreet" alone because it
pre-empts the obvious objection. Discreet on its own invites *so it is weak*. **Enough** answers it.

The structure is sufficiency, not superiority:

- Competitors argue **more**. More cable, more steel, more hardware.
- We argue **enough**, and that the excess is what you pay for in bulk you carry all day.

So the Black clip is **the everyday clip**: enough for safety, not so much that you notice it. Pro is
for when enough is more.

Phrasings that carry it:
- Enough to catch it. Not enough to notice it.
- Everyday carry, not equipment.
- You do not need a cable. You need enough.

## The boundary on that claim, and why it matters more than it looks

**"Enough for safety" is a substantiable claim, and right now we cannot substantiate it.**

Our largest fixable return reason is buyers reporting the product was weaker than they expected. Our
own word *retractable* manufactured part of that. If "enough" is published without a number behind
it, we repeat the same mistake in a new sentence and the returns follow.

So the claim ships in this order, never out of it:
1. Measure the load rating for Black and for Pro.
2. Publish the number.
3. Then say **enough**, with the number visible in the same frame.

The line is right. It just cannot go out ahead of the measurement.

# The second pillar: warranty

Discreet is why someone buys the product. **Warranty is why they risk buying it from us.** Those are
two different objections and they need two different answers.

## The objection warranty actually answers

We sit at **3.8 stars on 480 reviews**. Rogue is 4.6 on 2,300. A shopper comparing the two is not
weighing cord length, they are weighing *what happens to me if this one is a dud*. Nothing in the
listing currently answers that.

**One year, and two replacement clips, at $9.49.** Rogue is $21.99 and does not lead with a
warranty. Neither does BPZZ or SUPBEE. It is the strongest unclaimed trust asset we have, and it
costs us almost nothing on a $0.62 unit.

The pairing is the whole pitch: **you will not notice it, and if it fails we replace it.** The first
half sells the product, the second half neutralises the rating.

## Where it can and cannot appear

| Surface | Allowed | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Title | **No** | Amazon bans "warranty" and "guarantee" in titles outright. Recorded as PACR 5 |
| Bullet 5 | Yes | Already written in `listing-copy-2026-08-03.md` |
| Images | Yes | Deserves its own slot. Megan's brief already called for a One-Year Warranty image |
| A+ content | Yes | Its own module, near the end, where the trust objection lands |
| Sponsored Brands video | Yes | State the term plainly. No language implying Amazon backs it |

## How to say it

Plain and specific beats bold. **"One year. Two replacement clips. Email us."** A precise, small,
obviously-real promise reads as more credible than "lifetime guarantee", which reads as marketing
and which Amazon would also object to.

Never imply Amazon administers it. Never use the word guarantee.

## Per SKU

Both pillars run through every SKU. Discreet is the Black story and capacity is the Pro story, but
the warranty is identical across all four and should be stated identically across all four.

| SKU | Pole | Lead claim | Never say |
|---|---|---|---|
| Black Single (flagship, `57-P4AJ-J4AC`) | **Discreet** | Disappears when you are not using it | lightweight, heavy duty, tactical |
| Black 2-Pack (`CPH-BLCK-2`) | **Discreet** | Two, so it is already on the bag you grab | lightweight |
| Black 3-Pack (`CPH-BLCK-3`) | **Discreet** | One per bag, never move it again | lightweight |
| **Pro** (`UG-SVG8-LB0P`) | **Capacity** | Heavier phones, bigger cases | discreet, slim |

Black and Pro are deliberately opposite poles. Pro is where the customer who wants to *feel* the
hardware goes, and it is the honest answer for heavy phones. Do not blur them. The ~1,500 black
units on hand are the discreet story; the 545 Pro units are the capacity story.

## What changes in the creative

**Images.** We have no asset showing the clip WORN and not noticeable. That is the single missing
photograph and it is the one that proves the whole position. Needed: clip attached under a shirt
hem or inside a bag strap, cord retracted, barely findable in frame. Competitors cannot shoot this
because their product does not do it.

**Video.** The beat is *reach for the phone, use it, let go, it is gone again*. Not the catch. The
disappearance. The catch is the insurance; the discretion is the daily experience.

**A+.** The three-year-old document sells the object. The rebuild sells the absence of the object.
Module order: what it does, where it disappears, what happens when you drop, who it fits, why the
spring is soft, Pro comparison, **then the warranty as the closing module.** Trust is the last
objection a shopper resolves, so it goes last.

**Words to retire:** lightweight, tactical, heavy duty (on Black), rugged, military.
**Words to use:** disappears, out of the way, nothing hanging off you, you stop noticing it,
there when you need it.

## Compliance guardrails on the review-driven creative

William's 2026-08-06 direction was AI testimonials acting out real Amazon reviews, labelled as real
reviews. Two rules block that specific form:

1. **Amazon A+ policy prohibits customer reviews inside A+ content.** Already recorded as PACR 4 for
   listing images and Sponsored Brands video. It applies to A+ too.
2. **The FTC's 2024 final rule bans fake and AI-generated testimonials.** An AI actor performing a
   real customer's words, presented as that customer, is the exact fact pattern it targets.

**The version that ships:** the same themes, sourced from the 36 verified 5-star reviews
(dropping 14, work 13, theft 10, quality 10, peace of mind 6), spoken as our own benefit claims in
our own voice, with no attribution and no "real review" label. We keep the insight and drop the
impersonation. Attributed quotes remain OFF-Amazon only, and only from real named customers who
agreed.

## Open, and blocking

**The weight rating for BLACK and PRO.** Still unmeasured since June. It decides where the
discreet/capacity line falls between the two SKUs, and therefore which of the ~2,000 units on hand
each piece of creative is selling. Everything above is written to survive either answer, but the
comparison asset cannot be built without it.
